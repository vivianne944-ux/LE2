If You Only Remember These

  1. Central presynaptic α2A agonism → ↓ norepinephrine release → ↓ central sympathetic outflow → ↓ blood pressure. Page 2
  2. Clonidine withdrawal after prolonged high-dose use can cause rebound hypertension or hypertensive crisis. Pages 2–3
  3. When α and β blockade are both needed in rebound states or pheochromocytoma, give the α blocker first. Pages 3, 5
  4. Methyldopa is the lecture's preferred drug for hypertension during pregnancy. Page 3
  5. α1 blockade → vasodilation → ↓ PVR → ↓ BP. This can trigger orthostatic hypotension, reflex tachycardia, and fluid retention. Pages 1, 4
  6. Blocking presynaptic α2 removes autoinhibition → ↑ NE release → cardiac β1 stimulation → ↑ HR and ↑ renin release. Page 4
  7. Epinephrine reversal after α blockade is due to unopposed β2-mediated vasodilation → ↓ PVR → ↓ BP. Pages 4–5
  8. Phentolamine = reversible nonselective α blocker, α1 = α2 affinity, rapid and short acting, given IV or IM. Page 4
  9. Phenoxybenzamine = irreversible covalent nonselective α blocker, α1 > α2 affinity about 100:1, long acting. Pages 4–5
  10. Phenoxybenzamine is used before pheochromocytoma surgery, usually for 1–3 weeks. Page 5
  11. Prazosin, terazosin, doxazosin favor α1 over α2 about 1000:1, so reflex tachycardia is relatively minimal. Pages 5–6
  12. First-dose effect = marked postural hypotension and possible syncope after the first α1 blocker dose. Prazosin may cause it within 30–90 min. Pages 5–6
  13. Tamsulosin, alfuzosin, silodosin are the lecture's prostate-focused α1 blockers for BPH, especially when hypertension is absent. Pages 5–6
  14. Tamsulosin and silodosin are linked to abnormal or retrograde ejaculation and intraoperative floppy iris syndrome. Page 6
  15. Alfuzosin can increase QT-prolongation risk and should not be combined with CYP3A4 inhibitors listed in the lecture. Page 6
  16. BPH relief by α1 blockade: relax prostate and bladder neck smooth muscle → ↓ resistance to urine outflow → ↑ urine flow. Page 4
  17. IV clonidine can briefly raise BP through postsynaptic α2B vasoconstriction, then produce a longer hypotensive phase through central sympatholysis. Page 2
  18. Yohimbine blocks presynaptic α2 → ↑ NE → ↑ BP and HR, and is contraindicated in hypertension, angina, and renal impairment. Page 6

Where α2 receptors act in this lecture

  • Peripheral presynaptic α2 at adrenergic nerve endings → modulates neurotransmitter release.
  • Peripheral postsynaptic α2B on vascular smooth muscle → vasoconstriction.
  • Central presynaptic α2A in brainstem and medulla → ↓ sympathetic outflow from the vasomotor center → ↓ BP.
  • α2 activation in medullary salivation centers → dry mouth.

↓ heart rate, cardiac output, vascular tone, and blood pressure

Common adverse effects: drowsiness or sedation, xerostomia, ↓ BP, bradycardia, and withdrawal or rebound syndrome after abrupt cessation of chronic high-dose therapy.

Common uses: hypertension with clonidine, methyldopa, guanfacine. Glaucoma or ocular hypertension with apraclonidine and brimonidine.

Clonidine

MUST KNOW
Pharmacokinetics
Imidazoline derivative, lipid soluble, oral BA 95%, rapidly enters CNS, peak 1–3 h, t½ 8–12 h.
Main MOA
Central α2A activation → ↓ NE release → ↓ central sympathetic outflow → ↓ BP. Peripheral presynaptic α2 activation also suppresses NE, ATP, and NPY release. May activate I1 receptors. ↓ adenylyl cyclase → ↓ cAMP.
CV effects
↓ HR and stroke volume, relaxation of capacitance vessels → ↓ CO and PVR → ↓ arterial BP. The lecturer notes clonidine lowers HR and CO more than methyldopa.
Major adverse effects
Dry mouth and sedation are dose-dependent and reported in about 50% of patients. Delayed or retrograde ejaculation and marked bradycardia may occur.
Major use
Hypertension.
IV clonidine has a biphasic BP effect
Early
Postsynaptic α2B on vascular smooth muscle → transient vasoconstriction → acute ↑ BP.
Later
Central presynaptic α2A activation → prolonged ↓ sympathetic outflow → hypotension.
Oral dosing
The lecture says the hypertensive response is generally not seen orally.
EXAM TRAP
Do not confuse central α2A activation with peripheral postsynaptic α2B activation. One lowers sympathetic outflow and BP. The other can cause direct vasoconstriction.
SHOULD KNOW
  • Transdermal clonidine causes less sedation than tablets. One patch application reduces BP for 7 days. After removal, plasma concentration remains stable for about 8 h.
  • Renal: ↓ renin release through ↓ adenylyl cyclase and ↓ cAMP.
  • Eyes: ↓ aqueous humor production → ↓ intraocular pressure.
  • Avoid in patients at risk for mental depression. TCAs can block clonidine's antihypertensive effect according to the lecture.
  • Less common effects: vivid dreams, nightmares, restlessness, mental depression, and contact dermatitis with transdermal use.

Clonidine withdrawal

MUST KNOW
Abrupt discontinuation after prolonged use with high dose >1 mg/day
↓
Headache, nervousness, apprehension, tremor, abdominal pain, sweating, tachycardia
↓
Rebound hypertension or hypertensive crisis

Treatment in the lecture: reinstitute oral clonidine slowly at the minimal effective dose. If α and β blockers are used together, administer the α blocker first. Labetalol or carvedilol can supply β blockade with α-blocking properties. Sodium nitroprusside is listed for a faster, readily titratable effect.

EXAM TRAP
Giving a β blocker alone or before adequate α blockade can leave vascular α activity unopposed and worsen vasoconstriction and BP.

Methyldopa

MUST KNOW
  • L-DOPA analog. Converted to α-methyldopamine and α-methylnorepinephrine, which act as false neurotransmitters in peripheral neurons.
  • α-methyl NE stimulates presynaptic α2 receptors in brainstem → ↓ NE release → ↓ central sympathetic outflow → ↓ BP.
  • ↓ PVR with little change in CO and HR in typical patients.
  • Preferred drug for hypertension during pregnancy based on lecture-stated maternal and fetal effectiveness and safety.
SHOULD KNOW
  • BA 25%, t½ about 2 h, maximal effect 4–6 h after oral dose, action can persist up to 24 h because stored metabolites remain in adrenergic vesicles.
  • Prolonged use may cause salt and water retention or “pseudo-tolerance,” which may be overcome with diuretics.
  • Adverse effects: sedation, dry mouth, mental lassitude, impaired concentration, depression, nightmares, vertigo, EPS, decreased libido, and lactation in men and women.
  • Positive Coombs test may occur in 10–20% after more than 12 months, rarely with hemolytic anemia. Hepatotoxicity may occur with drug fever.

Phentolamine details

SHOULD KNOW
  • Inhibits responses to serotonin, stimulates muscarinic H1 and H2 receptors in the lecture's wording, facilitates insulin release, stimulates GI smooth muscle contraction and gastric acid secretion.
  • GI adverse effects: nausea, abdominal pain, peptic ulcer exacerbation.
  • Contraindications listed: coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction history, peptic ulcer or acid peptic disease.
  • May cause retrograde ejaculation and nasal congestion.

Phenoxybenzamine details

SHOULD KNOW
  • Also inhibits neuronal and extraneuronal NE uptake by inhibiting NET, reducing NE clearance.
  • Slow IV: sedation, fatigue, nausea. Rapid IV: CNS excitation and increased motor activity.
  • High doses irreversibly inhibit responses to serotonin, acetylcholine, and histamine.
  • May cause nasal stuffiness, retrograde ejaculation. The lecture notes mutagenicity in the Ames test and experimental animal studies.
SOURCE WORDING ALERT
On page 5, the phenoxybenzamine section says a “marked increase in blood pressure activates RAAS” while earlier pages describe decreased BP as the trigger for renin and RAAS activation. The PDF does not reconcile this inconsistency.

  • t½ 0.25–2.5 h. Readily enters the CNS and may cause tremors from CNS stimulation.

RECALL CHECK
Why does yohimbine raise HR and BP?
It blocks presynaptic α2, removes autoinhibition, and increases NE release and central sympathetic outflow.

Commonly Confused Concepts

Do not confuse: α2 agonist vs α2 antagonist
α2 agonist → ↓ NE release and ↓ sympathetic outflow. α2 antagonist such as yohimbine → removes autoinhibition → ↑ NE release.
Do not confuse: phentolamine vs phenoxybenzamine
Phentolamine = reversible, α1 = α2, short acting. Phenoxybenzamine = irreversible covalent, α1 > α2, long acting and pre-op pheochromocytoma.
Do not confuse: α1ABD blockers vs prostate-focused α1 blockers
Prazosin, terazosin, doxazosin are used when BP lowering is useful. Alfuzosin, tamsulosin, silodosin are emphasized for BPH when hypertension is absent.
Do not confuse: apraclonidine vs brimonidine
Apraclonidine does not cross the BBB. Brimonidine crosses the BBB and can cause mild hypotension and sedation.
Do not confuse: epinephrine after α blockade vs norepinephrine after α blockade
Epinephrine leaves β2 vasodilation unopposed, causing epinephrine reversal. Norepinephrine leaves β1 cardiac stimulation unopposed after its α1 effect is blocked.

Exam Traps

  • Alpha first, beta later in pheochromocytoma and when combining α and β blockade for clonidine rebound.
  • First-dose effect means a drop in BP with postural hypotension and syncope, not tachycardia as the defining event.
  • Epinephrine reversal = β2, not α1 or β1.
  • Phenoxybenzamine binds covalently and is irreversible. Phentolamine does not.
  • Tamsulosin is the classic lecture answer for IFIS and is less likely to cause first-dose hypotension than quinazolines.
  • Methyldopa is the lecture's preferred antihypertensive in pregnancy.
  • IV clonidine can briefly increase BP before the longer central hypotensive phase.
  • Selective α1 blockers cause less tachycardia because presynaptic α2 autoinhibition is preserved.

Last-Minute 5-Minute Cram Sheet

CORE MECHANISMS
  • Central α2A agonist → ↓ NE → ↓ sympathetic outflow → ↓ BP.
  • α1 blocker → vasodilation → ↓ PVR → ↓ BP → baroreflex + RAAS.
  • α2 blocker → remove autoinhibition → ↑ NE → ↑ HR and renin.
  • Epinephrine + α blocker → unopposed β2 → vasodilation → ↓ BP.
  • BPH: α1 blockade → relax prostate and bladder neck → ↑ urine flow.
DRUG ANCHORS
  • Clonidine: rebound hypertension if stopped abruptly.
  • Methyldopa: pregnancy, false neurotransmitters, Coombs positivity.
  • Phentolamine: reversible, short, α1 = α2.
  • Phenoxybenzamine: irreversible covalent, α1 > α2, pre-op pheochromocytoma.
  • Tamsulosin: BPH, IFIS, ejaculation abnormality.
  • Alfuzosin: QT and CYP3A4 concern.
  • Yohimbine: α2 blocker → ↑ NE, BP, HR.
SEQUENCES YOU MUST GET RIGHT
  • Pheochromocytoma: α blockade first → β blocker later if needed.
  • Clonidine rebound with combined blockade: α first.
  • IV clonidine: brief α2B vasoconstriction and ↑ BP → prolonged central α2A hypotension.
  • First-dose effect prevention: low first dose, bedtime, slow titration.

  • Apraclonidine: does not cross BBB.
