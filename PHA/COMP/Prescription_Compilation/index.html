1. How the quiz is likely testing you

Assume you may need to choose the drug yourself. The past-year questions give infection clues, organism clues, local resistance, allergy/comorbidity, then ask for a “rational” antimicrobial. Your lectures explicitly train you to select an antimicrobial from a clinical case.

Step 1 — Name the infection

Local vs systemic? Typical vs atypical? Superficial vs invasive? New TB vs retreatment? Uncomplicated vs severe?

Step 2 — Identify the likely organism

Gram stain, morphology, site, epidemiology, species, life-cycle stage, or TB resistance classification.

Step 3 — Eliminate unsafe choices

Allergy, pregnancy, G6PD, renal function, liver disease, age, QT/seizure risk, major interactions.

Step 4 — Choose the narrowest rational agent

Use local resistance and culture data. Empiric broad therapy may be needed in severe disease, then de-escalate.

Step 5 — Write every Rx element

Generic drug + dosage form + strength → quantity → clear label with route, frequency, duration, special instructions.

Step 6 — Check quantity

Quantity = doses/day × treatment days. For non-daily schedules, count each scheduled dose. FDC TB uses weight-band tablet counts.
