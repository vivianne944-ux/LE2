🚨 If You Only Remember These

  1. Baltimore classification is based on genome type + replication strategy. pp 1–2
  2. +ssRNA can act as mRNA immediately. Rubella and coronaviruses can translate soon after uncoating. pp 2, 4
  3. −ssRNA must first be transcribed into +mRNA by viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. pp 6–7
  4. RNA viruses replicate in the cytoplasm except influenza in the lecture rule. p 4
  5. Rubella rash appears with antibody, supporting an immune basis. IgG persists for life. p 3
  6. Congenital rubella classic triad = congenital heart disease + ocular defects + hearing loss. Earlier fetal infection means greater damage. pp 3–4
  7. Infant rubella IgM means fetal synthesis because maternal IgM does not cross the placenta. p 4
  8. SARS-CoV uses ACE2. MERS-CoV uses DPP4/CD26. HCoV-229E uses aminopeptidase N. p 4
  9. Severe COVID is associated with cytokine storm, lymphopenia, ARDS, coagulopathy, septic shock, and organ failure. p 5
  10. RT-PCR for an RNA virus needs reverse transcription because PCR amplifies DNA, not RNA. p 6
  11. Paramyxoviridae = enveloped, nonsegmented, negative-sense ssRNA. F0 must be cleaved to active F1 for membrane fusion. pp 6–7
  12. Syncytium formation comes from activated fusion protein causing neighboring cell membranes to fuse. pp 7–8
  13. Parainfluenza 1 and 2 → croup. Type 3 → bronchiolitis/pneumonia in younger infants. p 8
  14. Mumps has one antigenic type, lifelong immunity after infection, and can cause orchitis after puberty. pp 8–9
  15. Measles rash is immune-mediated. Rubeola is measles, not rubella. pp 9–10, review p 19
  16. Nipah is linked to fruit bats, pigs, contaminated fruit/raw date palm sap, and person-to-person spread. Hendra is classically bat → horse → human. pp 10–11
  17. RSV targets ciliated airway cells. Sloughed cells and debris obstruct small airways. pp 11–12
  18. Influenza has 8 segments. Segmentation enables reassortment. Shift = reassortment. Drift = gradual mutation. pp 13–16
  19. Influenza HA binds cells and is the major protective antigen. NA helps virion release by cleaving sialic acid. p 15
  20. Influenza A/B/C type is based on internal NP and M antigens, while Influenza A subtype is based on HA and NA. p 14

Genome rules emphasized in the lecture

  • DNA is usually double-stranded. Parvovirus is the ssDNA exception in the lecture. p 1
  • All single-stranded DNA viruses are described here as naked/non-enveloped. p 1
  • RNA is usually single-stranded. Reoviridae is the dsRNA exception. pp 1–2
  • RNA can also be classified by positive vs negative sense, segmented vs nonsegmented, and enveloped vs naked. p 2

Should knowEpidemiologic groupings

  • Enteric → usually ingestion/fecal-oral, primary intestinal replication.
  • Respiratory → inhalation or fomites, primary respiratory replication.
  • Arboviruses → replicate in blood-feeding arthropods before vertebrate transmission.
  • Blood-borne → transfusion, shared injecting equipment, parenteral body-fluid transfer, sometimes sexual contact.
  • Oncogenic → persistent infection may transform host cells and progress to malignancy.

Active recall · What is the cleanest difference between Baltimore Group IV and V?
Group IV +ssRNA can act as mRNA after uncoating. Group V −ssRNA must first be transcribed into +mRNA by viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
2 · Rubella Virus
Postnatal infection + congenital rubella syndrome · pages 2–4
pp 2–4
Must knowRubella is +ssRNA, so its genome can function immediately as mRNA. It is the sole member of Rubivirus in Matonaviridae, is not arthropod-transmitted, and has one serotype. p 2

Postnatal rubella pathogenesis

1 · ExposureDirect or droplet contact with respiratory secretions
→
2 · Local replicationRespiratory tract and URT mucosa
→
3 · Lymph nodesCervical lymph node multiplication
→
4 · ViremiaAbout days 7–9
→
5 · Antibody + rashAntibody appears around days 13–15 and coincides with rash
Clinical pattern
Malaise + low-grade fever + morbilliform rash. Rash starts on the face, spreads to trunk and extremities, and rarely lasts more than 3 days. About 20–50% of primary infections are subclinical. p 3

Immunity + serology

  • IgM appears early and does not persist beyond about 6 weeks. p 3
  • IgG persists for life. One attack gives lifelong immunity because there is one antigenic type. p 3
  • Maternal antibody protects offspring for about 4–6 months. p 3
  • Specimens listed include NP/throat swab, urine, IgM serum, IgG serum. p 3

Congenital rubella syndrome

Classic triad: congenital heart disease + ocular defects + hearing loss. p 3

Timing matters. Earlier infection, especially during organogenesis in the first trimester, produces greater fetal damage. After the 20th week, birth defects are uncommon. p 3

Exam favoriteInfant rubella-specific IgM = fetal infection evidence. Maternal IgM does not cross the placenta, so infant IgM must have been synthesized by the infant in utero. Maternal IgG can cross and is lost over roughly 6 months. p 4
Should knowRubella vaccine is live attenuated. The lecture notes more than 95% long-lasting immunity after one dose and lists monovalent, MR, MMR, and MMRV formulations. p 4
Active recall · Why does rash timing suggest an immune basis in postnatal rubella?
The appearance of antibody coincides with the appearance of the rash. That timing supports an immunologic contribution rather than rash being simply direct cell destruction.
Active recall · What single lab clue separates congenital rubella infection from passive maternal antibody?
Rubella-specific IgM in the infant, because maternal IgM does not cross the placenta.

4 · Paramyxoviridae Core Mechanisms
Negative sense, F0→F1 fusion, budding, stability, syncytia · pages 6–8
pp 6–8
Must knowParamyxoviridae = single-stranded, negative-sense, nonsegmented, enveloped. Most members are monotypic and are described as antigenically stable. p 6

Entry + replication

AttachmentHN, H, or G glycoprotein binds host
→
F0 cleavageHost protease cleaves F0
→
F1 activeNeutral-pH membrane fusion
→
Nucleocapsid enters cytoplasmEndosomal internalization can be bypassed
→
Transcription first−RNA → +mRNA by viral RdRp
No F0 cleavage → no active F1 → no fusion → no virion penetration → no infection. This sequence is highly testable. p 7

Budding and release

  • Progeny nucleocapsids form in the cytoplasm and move to plasma membrane sites containing viral glycoprotein spikes.
  • M protein is essential for particle formation and links the envelope to the nucleocapsid.
  • Parainfluenza and mumps HN has neuraminidase activity, which is described as helping prevent self-aggregation.
Why no reassortment?
The genome is nonsegmented, so there are no separate genome segments to reshuffle during coinfection. p 7
Why antigenically stable in the lecture?
HN/H/G and F surface proteins show minimal long-term variation. The lecture notes that many polymerase errors are not tolerated because they compromise structural or functional viability. p 7

Syncytium formation

→

→

→
SyncytiumLarge multinucleated giant cell
Should knowAcidophilic cytoplasmic inclusions are regular viral synthesis sites. Measles is noted as an exception that can also produce intranuclear inclusions. pp 7–8
Active recall · What is the one-word reason paramyxoviruses cannot undergo reassortment?
Nonsegmented.

Must knowIn immunocompetent hosts, replication is limited mainly to respiratory epithelium and viremia is uncommon.

Most common complication stated: otitis media.

Should knowImmunity

  • Types 1, 2, and 3 are distinct serotypes and lack significant cross-neutralization.
  • Maternal serum antibodies do not reliably prevent infection or disease.
  • Reinfection can occur despite antibodies, but disease is usually modified to a milder upper respiratory infection.
  • Secretory IgA is emphasized as most important against reinfection, but it disappears within months.
Active recall · Which parainfluenza types scream “croup” and which type screams “lower respiratory disease in a younger infant”?
Types 1 and 2 → croup. Type 3 → bronchiolitis/pneumonia in younger infants.
6 · Mumps Virus
Clinical disease, orchitis, immunity, diagnosis · pages 8–9
pp 8–9

Clinical picture

  • About one third of infections are subclinical.
  • Parotid and other salivary gland swelling causes pain and trouble chewing.
  • CNS involvement may occur and aseptic meningitis may spontaneously resolve.
  • After puberty, testes and ovaries may be affected.
Orchitis mechanism
Mumps orchitis is often unilateral. Inflamed testes swell inside the tight tunica albuginea, so swelling produces marked pain. pp 8–9
Must knowOne antigenic type + no significant antigenic variation → permanent immunity after a single infection. Even subclinical infection can generate lifelong immunity.
Immune markerTiming/meaning in lecture
HN antibodyDevelops slowly, around 4 weeks after onset, and persists for years
NP nucleocapsid antibodyAppears earliest, around 3–7 days after symptoms, and is gone within about 6 months
IgMEarly illness through about 60 days → recent infection
IgGPast infection
Should knowDiagnosis can use RT-PCR. Serology includes ELISA/HI, and paired sera showing a fourfold rise in antibody titer is emphasized. Prevention uses live attenuated mumps virus in MMR.
Active recall · Why is mumps immunity usually lifelong?
It is antigenically stable and has only one antigenic type, so immunity after infection is durable.

8 · Nipah + Hendra
Reservoirs, transmission chains, neuro-respiratory disease · pages 10–11
pp 10–11
Nipah virus
Fruit bats are central. Human infection may follow direct contact with infected animals such as bats or pigs, consumption of contaminated fruit or raw date palm sap, or close contact with body fluids of an infected person. Encephalitis can appear later, with confusion, drowsiness, seizures, and possible rapid progression to coma.
Hendra virus
Classic chain: flying fox bat → horse → human. The natural reservoir is Pteropus flying foxes in Australia. Human illness can start with severe flu-like lung disease and may progress to encephalitis.

Must knowOrthomyxoviridae is a segmented, enveloped RNA virus family. Influenza encodes its genome across 8 segments. Segmentation permits reassortment when different viruses coinfect the same cell. pp 13–14

Should knowSerology

Antibodies to HA, NA, NP, and matrix proteins appear during infection. Anti-HA immunity correlates with resistance. Routine serology in the lecture uses HI and ELISA, with paired acute and convalescent sera because baseline influenza antibodies are common. p 18

Should knowPrevention

The lecture lists inactivated parenteral vaccine, inactivated quadrivalent intradermal vaccine, and live attenuated quadrivalent nasal vaccine. Children may receive influenza vaccination from 6 months of age and then yearly. p 18

Active recall · NP/M versus HA/NA — which one gives type and which one gives subtype?
NP + M internal proteins define Influenza type A/B/C. HA + NA surface glycoproteins define Influenza A subtype.
Active recall · HA does what at the beginning, NA does what at the end?
HA binds susceptible cells and supports entry/fusion after cleavage. NA cleaves sialic acid to facilitate release and prevent self-aggregation of progeny virions.

🪤 Commonly Confused + Exam Traps

+ssRNA ≠ “positive protein”
The “positive” refers to RNA sense. +ssRNA can function like mRNA and be translated by host ribosomes.
−ssRNA cannot be translated directly
It must first become +mRNA through viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
Rubella ≠ rubeola
Rubella is German measles. Rubeola is measles.
Maternal IgG vs infant IgM
IgG can cross placenta. Maternal IgM cannot. Infant rubella IgM therefore supports in-utero infection.
Parainfluenza 1/2 vs 3
1/2 → croup. 3 → bronchiolitis or pneumonia, especially younger infants.
Paramyxo vs Orthomyxo
Paramyxo nonsegmented and stable. Orthomyxo segmented and reassorts.
Influenza type vs subtype
Type A/B/C = NP and M. Type A subtype = HA and NA.
Drift vs shift
Drift = gradual mutation. Shift = reassortment of segments and a major antigenic jump.
HA vs NA
HA gets influenza attached/in. NA helps newly formed virions get out.
Nipah vs Hendra
Nipah = fruit bats/pigs/raw date palm sap/person-to-person. Hendra = flying fox → horse → human.
RSV vs hMPV
Symptoms overlap heavily. RSV’s classic mechanism here emphasizes ciliated-cell sloughing and small-airway obstruction.
PCR vs RT-PCR
PCR amplifies DNA. RNA viruses need a reverse-transcription step to make cDNA first.
