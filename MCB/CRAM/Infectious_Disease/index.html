Zoonotic origin MUST KNOW

  • About 60% of all human infectious diseases recognized so far are zoonotic in origin.
  • About 75% of emerging infectious diseases over the past three decades originated from animals.
  • Humans have been drawn closer to animals — around 70% to 80% keep pets at home, and exotic animals are eaten, served in restaurants, and sold as household pets.
EXAM TRAP

60% vs 75% are easy to swap. 60% is all human infectious diseases. 75% is the narrower group of emerging diseases in the last three decades.

HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY
  • Emerging = first appearance or rapid rise in cases or spread to new areas.
  • Re-emerging = previously controlled, now rising again, typically a vaccine-preventable disease.
  • Measles is the model re-emerging answer. HIV, SARS-CoV-2, and Ebola are the model emerging answers.
  • 60% of all human infectious diseases and 75% of recent emerging diseases are zoonotic.
  • Pin the years — SARS 2003, H1N1 2009, MERS 2012, H7N9 2013, SARS-CoV-2 2019.

MECHANISM

  • CCR5 gives partial protection against HIV. HLA alleles only influence susceptibility — they are the distractor on HIV questions.

  • Four buckets — human behavior, physical and environmental, ecologic, genetic and biologic.
  • CCR5 alleles → partial protection against HIV. No Duffy antigen → complete protection against P. vivax.
  • Blood group O → increased susceptibility to severe cholera.

Core idea

Herd immunity MUST KNOW

  • Herd immunity = protection of a whole population by vaccinating a certain percentage of that population.
  • If only some get vaccinated the virus spreads. If most get vaccinated spreading is contained, and the immunized protect the non-immune susceptible population.
  • You do not have to immunize 100% of the population.
  • Herd immunity threshold = how much of the population must be vaccinated to achieve herd protection. It dictates the vaccine coverage rate and is itself dictated by R₀.
MECHANISM
High R₀ (one case infects many) ↓ Higher herd immunity threshold required ↓ Higher vaccine coverage rate needed ↓ If coverage falls short → outbreak

Basic reproduction number (R₀)

  • Measures disease transmission potential.
  • The average number of secondary infections produced by one infected individual in a certain population.
  • In plain terms, the ability of one infected case to infect X other people.

HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY
  • Herd immunity protects the non-immune susceptible population without needing 100% coverage.
  • R₀ = average secondary infections from one case, and R₀ dictates the herd immunity threshold, which dictates the required coverage rate.
  • Measles R₀ 12–18, threshold 92–94% is the single most quotable pair in this section.
  • Smallpox eradicated 1980. Smallpox, diphtheria, and paralytic polio all reached a 100% decrease in the US.

Core idea

Vaccination through the National Immunization Program (Expanded Program on Immunization) dropped pertussis to almost zero cases per year. Since 2016 there has been a steady rise, ending in a declared outbreak in March 2024.

The 2024 outbreak profile MUST KNOW

  • Outbreak declared March 2024. As of March 26, 2024 the declared areas were Quezon City, Iloilo City, and Cavite province.
  • By April 16, 2024 the affected areas had widened, and the age pattern was 77% under 5 years old with 90% aged 6 months and below.

The shape of the curve, explained

MECHANISM
Vaccine introduced (NIP/EPI) → cases near zero, controlled up to 2016–2018 ↓ Dengvaxia issue around 2016 → public confidence falls → vaccination rates drop ↓ Susceptible pool grows → rise in infections ↓ 2020–2022 pandemic → children stay home, less exposure → artificially lower incidence ↓ 2024 → exposure resumes on top of a large unvaccinated pool → outbreak

Compared with the early 2000s the overall incidence has significantly increased, so the pandemic dip should not be read as improvement.

HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY
  • Resurgence is multifactorial — better diagnosis, strain evolution, waning acellular vaccine immunity, and gaps in coverage and schedule.
  • Acellular replaced cellular because of reactogenicity, and antibody titers wane 2 to 3 years after the acellular vaccine.
  • Philippines gives primary doses only, no boosters after infancy.
  • 2024 outbreak — 77% under 5 years, 90% aged 6 months and below, 75% under- or unvaccinated.
  • Declared areas as of March 26, 2024 — Quezon City, Iloilo City, Cavite.
  • The trigger for the decline in coverage was the Dengvaxia issue around 2016.

8Avian Influenza — H5N1, H7N9, H5N6

▶
Pages 7–9

Core idea

  • Also called fowl plague or bird flu. These viruses primarily infect poultry or bird species and are only occasionally transmitted to humans through direct or indirect contact with poultry.
  • Caused by Influenza subtype A, which is the primary cause. Subtype A causes pandemics and undergoes genetic reassortment because of its wide variety of hosts.
  • Highly pathogenic means avian influenza viruses with certain genetic traits that produce lethal disease outbreaks in poultry.
  • Reservoir — wild birds, water fowl, poultry.
  • Two novel influenza A viruses associated with severe human disease — A(H5N1) first reported in Hong Kong in 1997 and A(H7N9) first reported in China in 2013.

Why H5N1 is severe yet spreads poorly MUST KNOW

MECHANISM
H5N1 has higher tropism for receptors in the LOWER respiratory tract, not the upper tract ├── Virus replicates deep in the lung → severe illness and high mortality └── Little virus in the upper airway → poor expulsion → limited person-to-person transmissibility

Person-to-person transmission has been documented for H5N1, so "never transmitted between humans" is false. It is limited, not absent.

H5N1 history and spread

  • 1997 first recognized, followed later that year by a series of 18 human illnesses in children and young adults. This triggered a mass culling of poultry in Hong Kong to control bird-to-human transmission.
  • The responsible virus became known as A/Goose/Guangdong/1/96/H5N1, named for a goose first identified in Guangdong, China in 1996. All subsequent human H5N1 illnesses and animal outbreaks descend from this lineage.
  • 2003 to 2004 — re-emerged and spread to Europe and Africa.
  • Endemic in poultry in Bangladesh, China, Egypt, India, Indonesia, and Vietnam.
  • In the 2015 outbreak, most cases came from Egypt.
  • Case distribution may reflect poultry reservoirs, local agricultural practices, intensity of surveillance, and other unknown factors.

H7N9 epidemiologic features may also be an artifact — the trans states it is unclear whether they result from differences in exposure to the virus source, differences in population immunity, clinical presentation, and illness severity, or a surveillance artifact.

2017 Philippine A(H5N6) outbreak MUST KNOW

  • First avian influenza outbreak recorded in the Philippines, back in 2017.
  • April to September 2017, affecting 2 provinces in Central Luzon — Pampanga and Nueva Ecija.
  • 37,000 birds died from H5N6 and 600,000 birds were culled.
  • No documented human case or death.
  • Outbreak officially declared over in September 2017.
EXAM TRAP

The Philippine outbreak was H5N6, not H5N1 and not H7N9. It killed birds but produced zero human cases.

Mode of transmission

  • Direct contact — handling poultry or infected carcasses, then touching eyes, nose, or mouth.
  • Indirect contact — touching surfaces contaminated with bird feces.
  • Droplets or dust — viral particles aerosolized in the air after a bird flaps its wings, scratches, or shakes its head, then inhaled or deposited on mucosa.
  • Human-to-human infection is very rare but may occur through direct respiratory routes.

Prevention and control SHOULD KNOW

  • Avoid contact with sick or dying birds.
  • Wash hands with soap and water after contact with sick or dying birds or contaminated objects (soil, cages, eggs).
  • Use personal protective equipment when handling birds or carcasses.
  • Seek medical advice if exposed to birds suspected of being infected.
  • Avoid buying live chicken for home slaughter.
  • Cook poultry including eggs thoroughly before eating.

Factors that help avian influenza spread — bird migration, international trade in poultry and poultry products, and illegal bird transport.

HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY
  • Avian influenza is influenza subtype A, reservoir is wild birds, water fowl, poultry.
  • H5N1 — 1997, Hong Kong, 3-year-old child, CFR about 59%, young patients, highly pathogenic in poultry.
  • H7N9 — 2013, Shanghai, 87-year-old index case, median age 61, quadruple reassortant, low pathogenicity in poultry.
  • H5N1 severity plus poor spread is explained by tropism for the lower respiratory tract.
  • Philippines 2017 outbreak was H5N6 in Pampanga and Nueva Ecija with no human cases.

9Pandemic Influenza A/H1N1 (2009)

▶

Core idea

A reassortant swine-origin influenza A virus that spread globally in 2009. Contrast it with H5N1 — H1N1 spreads easily but kills rarely, H5N1 barely spreads but kills often.

Genetic make-up MUST KNOW

MECHANISM — origin of P/H1N1

Chikungunya virus MUST KNOW

  • Family Togaviridae, genus Alphavirus.
  • The name means "to become contorted", describing the stooped appearance of patients suffering joint pain.
  • 1952 — first reported in an outbreak in Africa (Southern Tanzania). Since then reported in over 60 countries across Asia, Africa, Europe, and America.

  • Reservoir — humans in Asia, monkeys in Africa.

Japanese encephalitis virus MUST KNOW

  • Family Flaviviridae, genus Flavivirus. Related to dengue, yellow fever, and West Nile viruses.
  • Vectors — Culex tritaeniorhynchus and Culex vishnui groups.
  • Main cause of viral encephalitis in Asia.
  • Endemic in 24 countries, mostly Southeast Asia and Western Pacific, putting more than 3 billion people at risk.
MECHANISM — hosts in the JE cycle

EXAM TRAP

Three host roles, three different answers. Water birds are the natural reservoir, pigs are the amplifying host, humans are accidental. Picking "pig" for reservoir is the classic miss.

Clinical manifestations MUST KNOW

  • Incubation period 5 to 15 days.
  • Clinical spectrum — simple febrile illness (flu-like) → aseptic meningitis → encephalitis → abrupt seizures eventually leading to coma → poliomyelitis-like acute flaccid paralysis.
  • Case fatality ratio 5 to 30%.
  • Of those who survive, 30 to 50% suffer lasting central nervous system damage such as motor paralysis, inability to speak, and hearing problems.
  • Up to 50% of survivors are left with neurologic and psychiatric sequelae.

Zika virus MUST KNOW

  • Family Flaviviridae, genus Flavivirus, vector Aedes spp.
  • 1947 identified in a rhesus monkey in the Zika forest of Uganda. 1948 recovered from the mosquito A. africanus. 1952 first human cases in Uganda and the United Republic of Tanzania.
  • 1960 to 1980 human cases confirmed through blood tests with no deaths or hospitalizations, but widespread human exposure.
  • 2007 first large Zika outbreak in humans on the Pacific Island of Yap, Micronesia.
  • March 29, 2015 Brazil reports an outbreak of rash illness with nearly 7,000 mild cases.
  • July 2015 associated with Guillain-Barré syndrome, a lower motor neuron disease presenting with acute flaccid paralysis, showing the neurotrophic affinity of the virus.
  • October 2015 reported association between Zika virus infection and microcephaly.
  • At present widely distributed in 86 countries.

  • Majority are asymptomatic.
  • Symptomatic patients have a mild, transient illness lasting 2 to 7 days — fever, rash, conjunctivitis, muscle and joint pains, headaches.

  • Chikungunya — Togaviridae, Alphavirus, Aedes, hallmark is severe polyarticular arthralgia lasting months to years, the feature that separates it from dengue.

  • Zika is the arbovirus with vertical and sexual transmission, and semen can stay positive for 7 months.

History and distribution SHOULD KNOW

  • 1999 — first recognized during an outbreak among pig farmers in Sungai Nipah, Malaysia, who developed encephalitis. Most human infections resulted from direct contact with sick pigs or their contaminated tissues.
  • Since then, yearly outbreaks occur in South Asia — Bangladesh, India, Malaysia, and Singapore, causing severe disease and death.

Risk factors

  • Drinking raw date palm sap — fruit bats mainly feed on date palm sap.
  • Healthcare setting — contact with secretions, excretions, blood, or tissue of infected patients.
  • Close contact with infected pigs or other animals.

Clinical manifestation MUST KNOW

  • Incubation period 4 to 21 days.
  • Spectrum from asymptomatic infection to fatal encephalitis.
  • Initial flu-like symptoms — fever, headache, myalgia, vomiting, sore throat.
  • Followed by dizziness, drowsiness, altered consciousness, and neurological symptoms indicating acute encephalitis.
  • Also atypical pneumonia and acute respiratory distress.
  • Case fatality rate 40 to 75%.

The 2014 Philippine outbreak MUST KNOW

  • Occurred in Sultan Kudarat in 2014, with 17 patients infected and laboratory confirmed.
  • Breakdown — 11 with acute encephalitis, of whom 9 died (82% case fatality rate), 5 with influenza-like illness, and 1 with meningitis.
  • Patients were adults aged 21 to 60, with a median incubation period of 8 days.
  • Most common routes were direct exposure to infected horses, contact with contaminated body fluids during slaughtering of sick horses, and consumption of undercooked horse meat.
  • The source of horse infection is unclear but the most likely source is fruit bats, which were reported near at least 1 of the 2 villages.
  • Human-to-human transmission was also documented in 5 cases.
EXAM TRAP

In the classic Malaysian outbreak the intermediate animal was the pig. In the 2014 Philippine outbreak it was the HORSE. Same virus family, different intermediate host, and the local outbreak is the one likely to be asked here.

Prevention and control SHOULD KNOW

  • For local farmers, reduce bat access to date palm sap and other fresh food products.
  • Wear proper protective gear when handling sick animals or their tissues and during slaughtering.
  • Travelers should avoid contact with bats, sick horses and pigs, and their excretions.
  • Do not consume fallen fruit, raw date palm sap, or products made from raw sap. If consumed, freshly collected date palm juice should be boiled and fruits thoroughly washed.
  • Follow standard precautions in healthcare settings as well as contact and droplet precautions.
  • A Hendra virus vaccine for HORSES is licensed in Australia with potential future benefits for preventing henipavirus infections in humans. There is no licensed vaccine for humans.
HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY
  • Paramyxoviridae, enveloped ssRNA, reservoir is the Pteropus fruit bat which is a symptomless carrier.
  • 6 species — only Hendra and Nipah are highly virulent in humans.
  • Nipah 1999, Sungai Nipah, Malaysia, pig farmers with encephalitis.
  • Incubation 4 to 21 days, CFR 40 to 75%.
  • Signature risk factor — drinking raw date palm sap.
  • Philippines 2014, Sultan Kudarat, 17 cases, horses, 82% CFR among the 11 encephalitis cases.
  • Vaccine exists only for horses in Australia, none for humans.

SARS-CoV MUST KNOW

  • November 2002 — first appeared in Guangdong, China.
  • February 2003 — global outbreak from different guests at a Hong Kong hotel. The source was a physician from Guangdong province.
  • Spread worldwide affecting 29 countries, resulting in 8,096 cases on 5 continents with 774 deaths and a case fatality rate of 9.6%.
  • Surveillance technology now allows this virus to still be detected and monitored.

Features of SARS MUST KNOW

  • Relatively long incubation period — mean 4.6 days, range 2 to 14 days.
  • Minimal transmissibility early in the course of illness. Viral shedding in respiratory secretions follows a crescendo-decrescendo pattern with peak titers on day 10.
  • High proportion of illnesses (21%) in health care workers.
  • Low-to-moderate basic reproductive number (2 to 4) secondary cases per average infectious case.
  • Super-spreader events facilitated by inpatient therapeutic procedures that promoted aerosolization, which accounted for much of the observed person-to-person transmission.
  • Rarity of asymptomatic infections.
  • Striking age-dependent case-fatality ratio — under 1% in those below 24 years old versus over 50% in those over 65.
  • Once infected, the virus can mutate in certain individuals and spread further as a totally new strain, which spreads fast because others are not immune to it. SARS-CoV-2 was identified as airborne and through direct contact as well.
EXAM TRAP

SARS sheds latest, peaking on day 10, while SARS-CoV-2 has its highest viral load at symptom onset or in the first week. That timing flip is why SARS was containable and COVID-19 was not.

MERS-CoV MUST KNOW

  • Limited to the Middle Eastern countries.
  • Formerly known as HCoV-EMC for Erasmus Medical Center in the Netherlands.
  • The first lineage C beta coronavirus infecting humans, with a close relationship to betacoronaviruses HKU4 and HKU5 found in bats.
  • July 2012 — a 60-year-old man from Saudi Arabia with fatal lower respiratory tract infection.

15Commonly Confused Concepts

COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: Emerging vs Re-emerging

  • Emerging — first appearance in a population, or rapid rise in cases or geographic spread. Clue — HIV, SARS-CoV-2, Ebola.
  • Re-emerging — was controlled, now rising again. Clue — vaccine-preventable diseases such as measles, pertussis, diphtheria.
  • Fast distinction: if the disease has an effective vaccine and cases are climbing because coverage fell, the answer is re-emerging.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: Eradication vs Elimination

  • Eradication — complete, permanent, worldwide reduction to zero. Clue — smallpox in 1980.
  • Elimination — zero or a very low defined target rate in a defined geographical area. Clue — Philippines certified polio-free in 2000.
  • Fast distinction: eradication is global and permanent, elimination is local and can allow a low target rate.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: Acellular vs Cellular pertussis vaccine

  • Cellular — the older one, replaced because it caused reactogenicity.
  • Acellular — the replacement, with waning immunity 2 to 3 years after the dose and reported resurgence of cases.
  • Fast distinction: reactogenicity killed the old vaccine, waning immunity haunts the new one.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: Wild poliovirus vs Sabin virus vs Vaccine-derived poliovirus

  • Wild — naturally occurring, causes paralysis. Type 1 most common, Type 2 eradicated 1999, Type 3 last seen Nov 2012.
  • Sabin — the live attenuated OPV strain, has not diverged enough to cause paralysis, normally shed and found in environmental surveillance.
  • VDPV — Sabin-like virus that mutated and regained the ability to cause paralysis. Types 1 and 3 need over 1% divergence, Type 2 needs over 0.6%.
  • Fast distinction: Sabin is shed but harmless, VDPV is Sabin that turned dangerous, wild is the original.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: A(H5N1) vs A(H7N9)

  • H5N1 — 1997, Hong Kong, 3-year-old child, CFR about 59%, affects the young, highly pathogenic in poultry, lower respiratory tract tropism.
  • H7N9 — 2013, Shanghai, 87-year-old index case, median age 61, low pathogenicity to poultry, quadruple reassortant.
  • Fast distinction: H5N1 kills birds and kills the young. H7N9 spares birds and hits the old.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: Chikungunya vs Dengue

  • Chikungunya — severe polyarticular arthralgia of the ankle, knee, wrist, and hand, lasting months to years and potentially debilitating.
  • Dengue — breakbone fever with hemorrhagic shock as the feared outcome.
  • Fast distinction: the joints are the tell. Long-lasting, multi-joint pain points to chikungunya.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: JE reservoir vs amplifying vs accidental host

  • Water birds (egrets, herons) — the natural reservoir and host of Culex.
  • Pigs — the amplifying host, where the virus replicates without causing disease in the pig.
  • Humans — the accidental host.
  • Fast distinction: birds hold it, pigs multiply it, humans catch it by accident.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: Nipah in Malaysia vs the 2014 Philippine outbreak

  • Malaysia 1999 — pig farmers in Sungai Nipah, intermediate animal was the pig.
  • Philippines 2014 — Sultan Kudarat, intermediate animal was the horse, through direct exposure, slaughtering, and eating undercooked horse meat.
  • Fast distinction: pigs in Malaysia, horses in the Philippines, fruit bats behind both.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: Monkeypox Clade I vs Clade II

  • Clade I — Congo Basin or Central African, more deadly and more infectious, CFR up to 11%, up to 7 generations, subclades Ia and Ib, with Ib detected beyond Africa as of August 2024.
  • Clade II — West African, less transmissible and less deadly, CFR up to 6%, subclades IIa and IIb, with IIb responsible for the 2022 global outbreak.
  • Fast distinction: lower clade number equals higher danger, but the milder Clade IIb is the one that went global in 2022.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: Monkeypox vs Varicella (chickenpox)

  • Monkeypox — lymphadenopathy in the febrile stage, lesions on face and extremities, lesions at one stage at a time.
  • Varicella — more truncal, lesions at many stages at the same time.
  • Fast distinction: lymphadenopathy plus one uniform lesion stage equals monkeypox.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: SARS vs MERS

  • SARS — bat reservoir, civet cat intermediate, 2002 Guangdong, 8,096 cases, CFR 9.6%, peak viral shedding day 10.
  • MERS — bat primary host, camel intermediate, 2012 Saudi Arabia, 2,628 cases, CFR 36%, human-to-human less likely.
  • Fast distinction: civet equals SARS, camel equals MERS. Fewer MERS cases, far deadlier per case.
COMMON CONFUSION

Do not confuse: VUM vs VOI vs VOC

  • VUM — effects only suspected, evidence unclear, plus community transmission in at least 2 countries within 2 to 4 weeks.
  • VOI — effects predicted or known, plus a growth advantage in more than 1 WHO region.
  • VOC — a VOI that also causes worse clinical severity, substantial health system impact, or a significant decrease in vaccine effectiveness.
  • Fast distinction: suspected, then known, then harmful. Each level builds on the one before it.

EXAM TRAP — measles is the classic re-emerging answer

In the trans concept checkpoint, the answer is measles. HIV, SARS-CoV-2, and Ebola are emerging, not re-emerging, because they were never previously controlled.

EXAM TRAP — partial vs complete genetic protection

CCR5 alleles give PARTIAL protection against HIV. Only the lack of the Duffy blood group gives COMPLETE protection, and only against Plasmodium vivax. HLA alleles merely influence susceptibility, and blood group O increases risk of severe cholera.

EXAM TRAP — highest R₀ is not influenza

Measles has the highest R₀ (12 to 18) and the highest threshold (92 to 94%). Influenza has the lowest R₀ (1.4 to 4) and the lowest threshold (30 to 75%) despite being the famous pandemic agent.

EXAM TRAP — the inactivated polio vaccine is the broader one

The inactivated vaccine does not cause VDPV and contains all three serotypes. The live vaccine after the 2015 bivalent switch contains only types 1 and 3, and type 2 is the one causing 86% of cVDPV.

EXAM TRAP — measles cases rose right after MCV1

The sharp rise in 1982 after MCV1 was due to enhanced surveillance plus low vaccine coverage, not vaccine failure and not vaccine-caused disease.

EXAM TRAP — the top reason for measles non-vaccination

The most common reason is not eligible for vaccination at about 30%. Fear of side effects (about 10%) and against belief (about 5%) are near the bottom.

EXAM TRAP — H5N1 person-to-person spread is limited, not impossible

Person-to-person transmission has been documented. The reason it is limited is the higher tropism for LOWER respiratory tract receptors, which also explains the severity.

EXAM TRAP — the Philippine avian influenza outbreak was H5N6

2017, Pampanga and Nueva Ecija, 37,000 birds died, 600,000 culled, no human case or death. Do not answer H5N1 or H7N9.

EXAM TRAP — the H1N1 age of death discrepancy

The written trans states 87% of deaths in the under-65 group, while the lecture video said the elderly group. The written value is under 65, consistent with H1N1 mostly affecting children and young adults.

EXAM TRAP — only JE uses Culex

Among the arboviruses tabulated, Japanese encephalitis is the only one with a Culex vector. Dengue, yellow fever, Zika, and chikungunya all use Aedes.

EXAM TRAP — chikungunya is the only non-flavivirus in the group

Chikungunya is Togaviridae, genus Alphavirus. JE, yellow fever, and Zika are flaviviruses.

EXAM TRAP — Zika persists in semen

Zika has been isolated from semen even 7 months after infection, and vertical transmission is in contrast with other vector-borne diseases. Most infections are asymptomatic.

EXAM TRAP — the henipavirus vaccine is for horses

A Hendra virus vaccine for horses is licensed in Australia. There is no licensed vaccine for humans.

EXAM TRAP — Reston Ebola in the Philippines caused no human disease

In 2009, 6 workers tested antibody-positive after contact with sick pigs but had no disease and no significant symptoms. Reston is not as great a threat as the other species, yet it stays a public health concern as a newly emerging disease in animals and humans.

EXAM TRAP — Ebola per this lecture has no vaccine

The trans states there are no vaccines to date for humans or animals and that awareness plus protective measures are the only way to reduce infection and death. Answer from the lecture.

EXAM TRAP — monkeypox primary host is the rope squirrel

Despite the name, the primary host is the rope squirrel, and susceptible species are primarily rodents and squirrels. The 1958 isolation was from monkeys in a Danish research laboratory.

EXAM TRAP — the mild mpox clade caused the big outbreak

Clade IIb (West African, the less deadly clade) caused the 2022 global outbreak. Clade I is the deadlier clade, and subclade Ib is the one that spread beyond Africa in August 2024.

EXAM TRAP — condoms do not prevent mpox

Even though transmission is mainly sexual (88.4%), condoms will not help because infection still occurs through close skin contact. Isolation ends only when all lesions have crusted.

EXAM TRAP — no mpox testing during incubation

No testing is done in the 5 to 21 day incubation period. Serology is a recovery-phase test and is not locally available. IgM turns positive days 4 to 56 after rash onset.

EXAM TRAP — shedding timing separates SARS from SARS-CoV-2

SARS peaks on day 10 with minimal early transmissibility and rare asymptomatic infection. SARS-CoV-2 peaks at symptom onset or the first week and has affinity for the upper respiratory tract. That is the whole containment story.

EXAM TRAP — PHEIC is not a pandemic

WHO declared a PHEIC on 30 Jan 2020 and a pandemic on 11 March 2020. For mpox, the PHEIC was declared July 23, 2022.

1. Definitions that unlock everything

  • Emerging — appeared for the first time, or existed before and is rapidly increasing in new cases or spreading to new geographic areas.
  • Re-emerging — previously controlled, now reported in increasing numbers again. The prototype group is vaccine-preventable diseases, driven by declining vaccine coverage.
  • Eradication — complete, permanent, worldwide reduction to zero. Elimination — zero or a low defined target rate in a defined geographic area.
  • Herd immunity — protecting a whole population by vaccinating a percentage of it. You do not need 100%.
  • R₀ — average number of secondary infections from one infected individual. It dictates the herd immunity threshold, which dictates the needed coverage rate.
  • Vector — an arthropod that transmits a virus from one vertebrate to another.

2. Numbers worth memorizing

  • Zoonotic origin — 60% of all human infectious diseases, 75% of emerging diseases in the last three decades.
  • Measles R₀ 12–18, threshold 92–94%. Pertussis 5–17 and 92–94%. Polio 2–20 and 50–95%. Influenza is lowest at 1.4–4 and 30–75%.

38. How long can Zika be isolated from semen?

Even 7 months after infection.

39. Main reservoir host of henipaviruses?

The Pteropus fruit bat, a symptomless carrier.

40. Which two henipavirus species are highly virulent?

Hendra and Nipah.

41. Signature dietary risk factor for Nipah?

Drinking raw date palm sap.

42. Which animal drove the 2014 Philippine henipavirus outbreak?

Horses — direct exposure, slaughtering sick horses, and eating undercooked horse meat, in Sultan Kudarat.

43. Nipah incubation period and case fatality rate?

Incubation 4 to 21 days, CFR 40 to 75%.

44. Which henipavirus vaccine exists and for whom?

A Hendra virus vaccine for horses, licensed in Australia. There is none for humans.

45. Five Ebola species?

Bundibugyo, Zaire, Sudan, Reston, Tai Forest.

46. Which Ebola species links to the Philippines, and what happened in 2009?

Ebola-Reston. Six workers tested antibody-positive after contact with sick pigs but had no disease or significant symptoms.

47. Ebola laboratory findings?

Low WBC count, low platelet count, elevated liver enzymes.

48. Ebola incubation period and case fatality rate?

Incubation 2 to 21 days, CFR up to 90%.

49. Family, subfamily, and genus of monkeypox virus?

Poxviridae, Chordopoxvirinae, Orthopoxvirus.

50. Which mpox clade is deadlier, and which caused the 2022 outbreak?

Clade I, the Central African clade, is deadlier. Clade IIb, a West African subclade, caused the 2022 outbreak.

51. Primary host of monkeypox?

The rope squirrel.

52. Feature that distinguishes monkeypox from chickenpox?

Lymphadenopathy in the febrile stage, plus lesions concentrated on face and extremities at one stage at a time.

53. Order of the monkeypox rash stages?

Macule, papule, vesicle, pustule, crust.

54. When is a monkeypox patient infectious?

From symptom onset or prodrome until crusting of all skin lesions.

55. Main transmission route of the current mpox Clade II outbreak?

Sexual — 88.4% mainly transmitted sexually, with 87.1% among men who have sex with men.

56. Diagnostic test during the mpox febrile stage?

PCR on tonsillar and nasopharyngeal swabs for viral nucleic acids.

57. Named antivirals for severe mpox?

Tecovirimat (TPOXX), cidofovir (Vistide), brincidofovir (Tembexa), and vaccinia immune globulin.

58. Mpox vaccine type and post-exposure window?

Replication-deficient attenuated live virus vaccine, Ankara strain (JYNNEOS, Imvamune, Imvanex), preferably within 4 days of exposure.

59. How protective is the smallpox vaccine against monkeypox?

85% protective, and unvaccinated individuals account for 80 to 96% of cases.

60. The four globally endemic human coronaviruses?

229E, NL63, HKU1, OC43.

61. Natural reservoir and intermediate host of SARS-CoV?

Bat is the natural reservoir, civet cat is the intermediate host.

62. SARS case numbers and case fatality rate?

8,096 cases across 29 countries with 774 deaths and a CFR of 9.6%.

63. When does SARS viral shedding peak?

Day 10 of illness, following a crescendo-decrescendo pattern.

64. Which coronavirus has camels as intermediate hosts?

MERS-CoV, with bats as the primary host.

65. MERS case fatality ratio and main country?

36%, with the majority of cases from Saudi Arabia.

66. Two key WHO dates for COVID-19?

Public health emergency of international concern on 30 January 2020, pandemic declared on 11 March 2020.

67. Which receptor does SARS-CoV-2 bind, and which variant enhances it?

ACE2, with the G614 variant in the S protein postulated to increase infectivity and transmissibility.

68. Closest known relative of SARS-CoV-2?

A horseshoe bat SARS-related coronavirus, RaTG13, at 96.2% similarity, with spike proteins nearly identical to a pangolin virus.

69. What turns a VOI into a VOC?

At least one of — detrimental changes in clinical disease severity, substantial impact on health system capacity, or a significant decrease in vaccine effectiveness against severe disease.

70. Current predominant VOC, dominant VOI, and most prevalent variant?

Omicron is the predominant VOC, JN.1 is the dominant VOI, and XEC is the most prevalent variant.
