Oncologic Emergencies

1 Pressure / blockage2 Metabolic3 From treatment Easy to mix upCram SheetRapid-Fire

Oncologic emergencies come in three groups: (1) a tumor pressing on or blocking something, (2) a tumor causing a hormone or metabolic problem (paraneoplastic), and (3) a complication of cancer treatment itself. p. 565

1. When the Tumor Presses on or Blocks Something

Superior Vena Cava (SVC) Syndrome Must knowpp. 565–566

What it is
The SVC gets blocked, so blood from the head, neck, and arms can't drain back to the heart properly.
Main cause
Lung cancer (especially small-cell and squamous) causes about 85% of cancer-related cases. In young adults, think lymphoma. Catheters and pacemaker wires are a growing non-cancer cause.
How it shows up
Swollen face and neck (especially around the eyes), shortness of breath, cough. Visible swollen neck veins and extra veins on the chest wall. Symptoms get worse when lying down or bending forward.
How to diagnose
It is mainly a clinical diagnosis. The chest x-ray usually shows a widened upper mediastinum (often on the right), but a normal x-ray doesn't rule it out. CT confirms it by showing the blocked vein and the detour (collateral) veins.
Treatment
Depends on the cancer type:
  • Non-small-cell lung cancer or other solid tumors → radiation
  • Small-cell lung cancer, lymphoma, germ cell tumor → chemotherapy (these respond well to chemo)
  • Severe symptoms or it keeps coming back → a stent placed inside the vein for fast relief
  • Caused by a catheter clot → remove the catheter and give blood thinners, or dissolve the clot early to save the catheter
Most dangerous partThe real emergency is when the mass squeezes the trachea (windpipe). That needs treatment right away.
Watch outA stent suddenly sends a lot of blood back to the heart, which can cause heart failure and fluid in the lungs. Also, patients die from the cancer itself, not from the SVC blockage.

Spinal Cord Compression Must know In past LEpp. 568–570

What it is
A tumor outside the cord (usually spread to a vertebra) grows and pushes on the spinal cord. Happens in 5–10% of cancer patients.
Why it happens
Cancer spreads to the vertebral body, it grows and presses on the cord. The pressure injures the nerves directly and cuts off their blood supply.
Who and where
Most common cause: lung cancer. Also breast, prostate, and multiple myeloma. Most common level: thoracic spine (70%), then lumbosacral (20%), then cervical (10%).
How it shows up
  • First symptom is back pain at one spot, often for days to months before anything else
  • Unlike a slipped disk, the pain is worse when lying flat
  • Later: leg weakness, numbness. Loss of bladder/bowel control comes late
What to do
See the steps below. MRI of the spine is the test of choice. Plain x-rays often miss it.
Cancer patient with back pain → do a neuro exam
↓ if it looks like cord compression
Give high-dose dexamethasone right away (reduces swelling)
↓
Get an MRI of the whole spine
↓ if tumor is pressing on the cord
Treat with radiation + steroids. Surgery first if the spine is unstable.
Why speed mattersPatients do best if treatment starts while they can still walk and control their bladder.
Past exam question (2027 LE 2)35/F with stage IV breast cancer spread to bone has low back pain and weakness in both legs. Best test? → MRI of the (lumbar) spine. X-ray can't show the cord, and a head CT or nerve study looks at the wrong thing.

Extra: the earliest x-ray sign is pedicle erosion ("winking owl" sign). About 20% of collapsed vertebrae are from osteoporosis, not cancer.

Fluid Around the Heart (Pericardial Effusion / Tamponade) Must knowpp. 566–567

What it is
Cancer spreads to the sac around the heart and fluid builds up. If enough fluid collects, it squeezes the heart (tamponade).
Main causes
Lung and breast cancer, leukemia, lymphoma. But in about half of cancer patients with symptoms, the cause is not the cancer (for example radiation, some drugs, low thyroid).
How it shows up
Most patients have no symptoms. If present: shortness of breath, cough, chest pain, trouble breathing when lying flat.
How to diagnose
Echocardiogram is the most helpful test. Checking the fluid for cancer cells (cytology) confirms it in most cases.
Treatment
If the patient is unstable → drain the fluid right away with a needle (pericardiocentesis). It comes back in about 1 in 5, so doctors may add a drug to seal the sac (sclerotherapy) or make a pericardial window. (Your 2.03 trans says the same: window, stripping, or chemical sclerosis.)
Watch outIf cancer cells are found in the fluid, survival is very short, about 7 weeks.

Brain Metastases and High Pressure in the Skull Must knowp. 570

Who gets it
Mostly from lung cancer, breast cancer, and melanoma. About 25% of cancer patients die with brain metastases.
How it shows up
Headache, nausea, vomiting, behavior change, seizures, weakness on one side. A sudden stroke-like start usually means the tumor bled (common with melanoma, germ cell, and kidney cancer).
Best test
MRI with contrast (better than CT, catches small lesions).
Treatment
  • First, for anyone with symptoms: dexamethasone
  • Many lesions → radiation to the whole brain
  • One lesion and the rest of the cancer is controlled → surgery + whole-brain radiation
  • 1 to 4 lesions → stereotactic radiosurgery (very focused radiation beams)
Watch outSteroids can weaken immunotherapy. If a patient on immunotherapy can't come off steroids, bevacizumab can be used for the brain swelling instead.

Seizures Must knowp. 571

Main cause
In cancer patients, the most common cause is cancer that spread to the brain. (But seizures happen more often with tumors that started in the brain.)
Treatment
Only treat once seizures have actually happened: phenytoin or levetiracetam. If that doesn't work, add valproic acid.
Preferred drug
Levetiracetam (or topiramate). Phenytoin speeds up liver enzymes (CYP450) and changes the levels of many chemo drugs. Levetiracetam doesn't.
Watch outDo not give anti-seizure drugs "just in case" (no prophylaxis). After brain surgery, any preventive seizure drug is stopped within the first week.

Leukostasis (Blood Too Thick With Leukemia Cells) Must knowp. 571

What it is
In acute leukemia, especially AML (acute myeloid leukemia), blast cells go above 100,000. The blood becomes thick, and the cells clog and break through small vessels, causing bleeding in the brain and lungs.
How it shows up
Brain: confusion, headache, dizziness, vision problems, coma. Lungs: shortness of breath, low oxygen.
Treatment
  1. Hydroxyurea to quickly lower the count while you work it up
  2. Start real leukemia chemotherapy soon
  3. Leukapheresis (machine filters out white cells) if there are symptoms
Watch outThe blood gas oxygen reading is falsely low because the white cells use up oxygen in the syringe. Use pulse oximetry instead. Also avoid dehydration and unnecessary transfusions, which make the blood even thicker.

Coughing Up Blood (Hemoptysis) Must knowpp. 571–572

Main cause
Lung cancer. Up to 20% of lung cancer patients cough up blood at some point.
"Massive"
More than 200–600 mL in 24 hours, or any amount that threatens life.
What to do
  1. Secure the airway, give oxygen, stabilize blood pressure
  2. If you know which lung is bleeding, lay the patient with the bleeding side down so blood doesn't flow into the good lung
  3. Still bleeding a lot → intubate and do an emergency bronchoscopy
  4. Main fix: bronchial artery embolization (plugging the bleeding artery). It works in 75–90%.

Extra: bevacizumab can cause deadly bleeding in squamous lung cancer.

Other Blockages Should knowpp. 567–568, 572

BlockageWhat to remember
BowelMostly colorectal and ovarian cancer. Crampy pain. A cecum wider than 12–14 cm can burst, so it's a surgical emergency.
UrinaryProstate and cervical cancer. Flank pain is the most common symptom. Kidney ultrasound is the safest and cheapest test.
Bile ductPancreas and bile duct cancer. Jaundice, itching. Only intervene if there's bad itching, malabsorption, or infection.
AirwayUsually lung cancer invading the airway. Blockage above the voice box → tracheostomy. Lower down → open it with a bronchoscope or stent.
Meninges (neoplastic meningitis)Cancer spreads to the brain's lining. Look for cancer cells in the spinal fluid, but it's missed up to 40% of the time, so repeat the spinal tap.

2. Metabolic Problems Caused by the Tumor

The Five to Know Must knowpp. 572–573

ProblemWhat to remember
High calciumThe most common paraneoplastic syndrome.
Low sodiumMost often from SIADH (too much antidiuretic hormone, so the body holds water).
Lactic acidosisRare. Usually when the liver is full of tumor. The acid is what's dangerous, not the lactate itself. Treat the cancer. Bicarbonate only if the acidosis is very severe.
Low blood sugarLarge tumors (not pancreatic) release a form of IGF-II that acts like insulin. Real insulin levels are low.
Adrenal insufficiencyEasy to miss because nausea, vomiting, and low blood pressure get blamed on the cancer. Can come from tumor in both adrenals, or drugs like megestrol and ipilimumab.
Adrenal crisisIt can kill. Draw blood for cortisol and ACTH first, then start treatment right away.
Watch outA very high white count can make blood sugar look falsely low because the cells use up the sugar in the tube (pseudohypoglycemia).

Immunotherapy and the Pituitary Should knowp. 573

  • Ipilimumab (anti-CTLA-4) can inflame the pituitary gland (hypophysitis) about 6–12 weeks after starting. Symptoms: headache, vision problems, low hormones. It's usually permanent, so patients need lifelong hormone replacement.
  • PD-1 / PD-L1 drugs rarely do this (~1%), but they inflame the adrenal gland more often than ipilimumab does.
  • Megestrol can shut down the adrenal gland, yet oddly can also cause Cushing's features.

3. Complications of Cancer Treatment

Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS) Must knowpp. 573–574

What it is
Chemo kills a huge number of fast-growing cancer cells at once. They burst and dump their insides into the blood.
The 4 lab changes
High uric acid, high potassium, high phosphate, low calcium. Kidney failure is common.
Who gets it
Burkitt's lymphoma and acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) most often. Usually during or 1–5 days after chemo.
Why the kidneys fail
Uric acid (from broken-down DNA) forms crystals in the kidney tubules. Phosphate binds calcium and forms calcium phosphate crystals, which also drops blood calcium. High potassium plus failing kidneys can cause deadly heart rhythms.
Prevention
Allopurinol + lots of IV fluids before chemo. Prevention is the most important step.
Other drugs
  • Febuxostat: use if allergic to allopurinol or kidneys are failing
  • Rasburicase: an enzyme that turns uric acid into a form that dissolves easily. Works within hours. For high-risk patients or when uric acid won't come down.
When to dialyze
Potassium above 6, uric acid above 10, creatinine above 10, phosphate above 10, or low calcium causing symptoms.
Watch out
  • Don't alkalinize the urine with bicarbonate anymore. It helps uric acid dissolve but makes other crystals (xanthine, calcium phosphate) form.
  • No rasburicase in G6PD deficiency (it produces hydrogen peroxide, which these patients can't handle).
  • Rasburicase keeps breaking down uric acid in the blood tube, so put the sample on ice or the result will be falsely low.

Antibody Infusion Reactions and Cytokine Release Syndrome Must knowpp. 574–575

What it is
The first dose of an antibody drug (like rituximab) makes immune cells release a flood of inflammatory chemicals (cytokines, especially IL-6). This is cytokine release syndrome (CRS).
How it shows up
Fever, chills, nausea, headache in up to half of patients. Rarely, low blood pressure, breathing failure, or shock.
CAR T cells
CRS is their most common sudden side effect.
Treatment
Severe CRS → tocilizumab, which blocks the IL-6 receptor. For a rituximab reaction: stop the drip, then restart at half the speed once symptoms settle.

Hemolytic-Uremic Syndrome (HUS) Must knowp. 575

What it is
Chemo damages the lining of small blood vessels, mainly in the kidneys. Tiny clots form, which shred red cells and use up platelets.
Main drugs
Mitomycin and gemcitabine. Usually 4–8 weeks after the last dose.
The triad
Red cells breaking apart (anemia with schistocytes on smear), low platelets, and kidney failure. The Coombs test is negative (not an antibody problem).
Treatment
Outlook is poor. Eculizumab is first line for atypical HUS, but patients must get the meningococcal vaccine first.
Watch outA blood transfusion can make HUS suddenly worse (high blood pressure, fluid in the lungs, more red cell breakdown).

Lung Damage From Treatment Must knowpp. 575–576

When a cancer patient has new lung shadows and shortness of breath, the most common cause is the treatment.

CauseClueWhat to do
Chemo drugs (e.g., bleomycin, methotrexate)Scarring and inflammation, low oxygenSupportive care, steroids may help. Give the lowest oxygen that keeps saturation OK, because extra oxygen makes the damage worse.
Immunotherapy (PD-1 / PD-L1 drugs)~10%. Hazy "ground glass" in the lower lungsStop the drug, give high-dose steroids
Radiation2–6 months after radiation, only inside the radiated areaPrednisone if symptomatic. Taper slowly, stopping suddenly makes it flare. No symptoms = no treatment needed.

Neutropenic Enterocolitis (Typhlitis) Must knowp. 577

What it is
The cecum (start of the large bowel) becomes inflamed and starts to die in patients with very low neutrophils, classically during acute leukemia treatment.
How it shows up
Right lower belly pain + fever + low neutrophils, often watery diarrhea.
Best clue on CT
Thick cecal wall. Air inside the bowel wall (pneumatosis intestinalis) is the most specific sign.
Treatment
Broad-spectrum antibiotics, nothing by mouth, nasogastric tube, and white-cell growth factors. Surgery only if the bowel perforates, dies, or bleeds.

Bleeding Bladder (Hemorrhagic Cystitis) Must knowp. 577

What it is
The bladder lining bleeds, mostly after cyclophosphamide or ifosfamide.
Why
These drugs break down into acrolein, which passes into the urine and irritates the bladder.
Prevention
Keep urine flowing (lots of fluids) and give mesna, which neutralizes acrolein.
If it happens
Keep urine flowing. If still bleeding, rinse the bladder with formalin.
After bone marrow transplant
Early = the drug. Late = a virus (BK virus or adenovirus).

Other Treatment Problems pp. 575, 577–578

  • Low neutrophils + infection is the most common serious complication of cancer treatment.
  • Allergic reactions to chemo: taxanes (like paclitaxel) react on the 1st or 2nd dose, so patients get pre-medicated. Platinum drugs react after many doses.
